## Scrap with a recognisable character

The most interesting task was making each resource look like a crooked, fused mass of recognisable objects while still reading as one material. I explored the shapes through generated image references and, for some models, physical simulations that brought the pieces together in irregular arrangements.

I gave the UVs and texturing more time than in Misform. The palette is a little brighter, with more sand, rust, dirt, and dust: I wanted the objects to feel worn and used.

## The workbench

The workbench is my favourite individual prop in this set, despite its simple form. It was the place where the player dismantled discarded items into resources.

I made these models around mid-September 2026, after first building the game with engine primitives. All nine models shown here were used in the prototype; the larger project remained unfinished.

## From discarded objects to resources

A plastic bottle yielded plastic, a copper cable yielded plastic and copper, and an aluminium can yielded aluminium. The player dismantled them at the workbench, collected the resulting materials in a container, and sold them.

The game was intended to grow from an old hangar and a small patch of land into a recycling enterprise, with new equipment, rearranged work areas, and gradual automation. These models supported the initial sorting and dismantling loop.

## Containers & surface work

I modelled and unwrapped the objects in Blender and textured them in Substance Painter. I started with supplied Substance materials and some lettering and sticker textures, then substantially adjusted and combined them. AI-generated images served as references for the forms.

I kept the geometry economical because the game was designed to have hundreds of these objects in a scene at once. The low-poly forms and pixelated textures remained the basis of the style.
