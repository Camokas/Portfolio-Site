## Editing for TikTok

These videos document my approach to platform-specific short-form editing: filmed monitor footage, a compact idea, quick cuts, colour grading, and text that can be understood immediately. Instead of squeezing a YouTube edit into another platform, I learned to build the video around TikTok's pace.

## A shorter idea, a faster cut

The new approach matched the platform more closely: quicker cuts, less spoken narration, trending music, and one concise idea or meme. Videos started taking off, and I kept refining pacing, colour grading, phrasing, and my feel for trends. It was a different creative skill from making a densely animated YouTube Short.

## Filming the screen instead of capturing it

I liked how quickly I could make and publish these videos. I filmed the monitor with a phone, so the hardest part was often the idea itself, the scene open on screen, and a clean, well-paced edit. That momentum even inspired me to buy a new smartphone with a camera I considered excellent at the time: image quality mattered directly to this way of filming.

By comparison, a YouTube Short of similar length could feel about five times more involved: OBS captures, a Premiere Pro assembly, elaborate After Effects animation, edits matched to music, voice processing in Audition, and a long Media Encoder render. The five-times comparison describes my experience of the workload, not a measured production benchmark.
