## Editing 107 Shorts and horizontal videos

I turned game development footage into short stories: a hook, an animated explanation, and a conclusion timed to the music. Alongside Shorts, I edited music visualisers and a trailer for Misform.

<StoryPanel kind="portfolio" group />

## A carefully assembled workflow

My YouTube Shorts were short in duration, but often demanding to produce. I captured multiple pieces of footage directly from the screen in OBS, assembled them in Premiere Pro, created explanatory animations and overlays in After Effects, aligned cuts to music, processed voice recordings in Adobe Audition, and rendered the result through Media Encoder.

The challenge was to make a complex development topic feel clear and lively within a tiny running time.

<Image index={3} group open />

## One creator, from script to render

I handled the complete production. AI occasionally helped develop ideas and scripts; I recorded the narration and created the visuals myself.

<StoryPanel kind="solo" group />

## Making regular production sustainable

Reusable tools and simpler animation setups reduced production time. That made daily or alternate-day publishing more manageable without giving up the visual explanations that defined the channel.

<StoryPanel kind="time" group />

## My signature: explaining through animation

My signature was carefully made After Effects animation and visual explanations of game systems. Some early Shorts became so complex that even the lowest-quality preview sometimes ran at about one frame per second. I learned to optimise editing itself: get as close as possible to the desired image with fewer resources, rather than keep adding effects.

<Image index={4} group open />
<Image index={5} group open />

## Presets and practical automation

I automated repetitive editing tasks and saved colour presets for the look I wanted from SDR recordings. Reusable setups helped make editing practical on limited hardware.

<StoryPanel kind="tools" group />

## Making the voice sound better

In Adobe Audition, I reduced echo and noise and adjusted the voice's tonal balance. Saved effect chains made it quicker to get consistent, intelligible narration from similar recordings.

<Image index={6} group open />

### Why trees are not blocks

39,344 · views at capture · 99 comments

<Video index={1} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

Why are trees in my game entities rather than blocks? I recently added the first stumps to IsLands and spent a long time thinking about how to implement them. Making them out of blocks felt boring and unoriginal, so I went a different way. My character is two-dimensional in a three-dimensional world: why not do the same with trees? It looked nice, but raised another question: how do you interact with them? The solution was to make them entities without movement. They are alive, like in reality, but stay in place. Chopping a tree is no longer breaking a block: it deals actual damage. A tree remembers its state and can remain partly chopped in the world. Do you think trees should regenerate over time? Join Telegram to try the new version and discuss ideas with the community.

</details>

### Someone else is making my game

24,549 · views at capture · 75 comments

<Video index={3} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

I recently realised that other people are already making the game I am working on. I came across a Steam project on YouTube with a similar idea: a voxel world, survival, and, most importantly, floating islands. I was shocked. Of course I cannot compete with a large studio and its resources, but I will not abandon my own path. IsLands is developing in its own direction, shaped by subscribers, player ideas, reported bugs, and suggested mechanics. That is our advantage: we move the game forward together. It is a funny situation, but we are definitely not stopping. Should an indie project keep going when a large studio is making something similar? Join the discussion in Telegram.

</details>

### Optimisation chosen by the community

24,150 · views at capture · 21 comments

<Video index={4} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

Optimisation was a community decision. The game worked, but every new feature made worlds load more slowly and reduced FPS. Playing was possible without optimisation, but not very enjoyable. I asked Telegram subscribers whether to continue optimising or stop at the basics. 87% said: do it properly now and make it last. I rewrote generation, distributed the workload, and sped up world loading. Islands used to appear in bursts; now the process is faster and more stable. The community helped decide what I should work on. Want to influence the next update? Join Telegram.

</details>

### A world that feels alive

19,001 · views at capture · 47 comments

<Video index={5} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

The world of my game is coming alive. Rabbits are no longer decorations: each has its own logic, personality, and behaviour. They eat, flee, fall, and recover, with varieties from ordinary field rabbits to rare golden ones. Butterflies bring no practical benefit, but make the scenery cosier. The campfire is now a complete system: light it, feed it fuel, put it out, or fire clay into bricks. A new material makes the next step towards building more deliberate. IsLands Alpha 0.4.6 is the update where the game first feels alive. A like is the best way to say “keep going”. Our Telegram community suggests ideas, discusses updates, and finds bugs. See you there!

</details>

### Randomness and your first tool

14,561 · views at capture · 28 comments

<Video index={6} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

I recently added even more randomness to the game. There was already plenty of it, but the amount and composition of drops from blocks and entities now vary. Instead of simply dropping a whole dirt block, the ground gives you handfuls that you craft back into one. The new forest litter block is particularly interesting: it can drop sticks and stones. Dirt almost always drops at least one, but litter may drop nothing. In theory, you could dig up all the litter in the world and never see a stone or stick; that would take extraordinary luck. Join Telegram and tell the community how many attempts it took to make your first tool. Why are trees considered entities in IsLands? I will explain in the next video.

</details>

### Why inventory is so difficult

14,319 · views at capture · 19 comments

<Video index={7} group open />

<details>
<summary>On-screen text · edited transcript and translation</summary>

Why is inventory the hardest part of developing my game? At first I had a list of items in code. Then I made a window with icons and numbers, and thought I was done. That was when the real trouble began. How should items be dragged? What if the player clicks the wrong place? How should identical items stack? Every new button or action broke something old. Even the right mouse button became a puzzle: pick up, put down, or drop? That ordinary little window turned out to be more complicated than half the game. Whenever you open a working inventory, remember that somewhere a developer has survived dozens of bugs.

</details>
