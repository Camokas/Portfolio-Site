## Editing for someone else's audience

I edited gameplay for two clients, mainly for horizontal YouTube videos. The work centred on choosing worthwhile moments and building a watchable sequence, with effects used where they helped. Depending on the brief, I either shaped the edit independently or followed a specific reference.

<StoryPanel kind="commissioned" group />

## Finding the video inside the footage

I reviewed recordings at 2–3× speed when the PC could handle it, marking useful moments before assembling the cut in Premiere Pro. I rearranged events where needed but preserved continuity, especially around speech. The opening needed particular care: it had to establish interest without confusing the viewer.

<Image index={2} group open />

## More than a long timeline

One of the most demanding orders combined an extensive gameplay recording with voice and animation. I took the material through selection, editing, sound, graphics, and export, using After Effects, Adobe Audition, Photoshop, and Media Encoder alongside Premiere Pro. Careful folder organisation helped keep complex projects manageable.

<Image index={1} group open />

## Refining the cut with the client

I sent a rough export for review, then adjusted individual cuts, pacing, and transitions. The preserved notes ask for a more natural camera change, a longer driving moment, and removal of a distracting insert. Another exchange covers smoothing audio between clips and praises the music and synchronisation.

## A client's Shorts channel

I managed a client's League of Legends channel, working from raw English-language footage. I translated speech as needed to select moments, edited and published videos, and prepared thumbnails, the avatar, banner, titles, and descriptions. The client approved the work. Shorts were additional to the estimated 20–25 long-form edits.

For the mascot, we chose a League of Legends character together. I generated frames with AI and animated them to speak and show emotions during the videos.

## Scope and workload

Most orders used the basic package: editing, sound work, and the source project. Some clients paid for extras. The screenshot preserves the service packages I offered at the time.

Orders took anywhere from half a day to several days. This period built my stamina for sustained selection and editing: keeping a long video clear and engaging without relying on elaborate effects. Afterwards, I shifted my focus to game development.
