## Two chapters of one channel

The channel grew from personal notes into a public game development diary. Opening it to viewers from YouTube and TikTok created a place to share work before release and discuss it directly with players.

<StoryPanel kind="telegram-period" group />

## A place to share and listen

Most conversations centred on IsLands. I shared test builds and work in progress, asked players about priorities, and used their responses to plan updates.

<StoryPanel kind="telegram-audience" group />

## The first public build notes

A July 2025 post describes block destruction, inventory, drops, crafting, textures, and controls. The channel gave an update a readable home beyond a short video: what changed, what could be tested, and how to try it.

<Image index={1} group open />

## Music as part of the project

A post celebrating 100 YouTube subscribers shared my own music. Development, sound, and the small milestones around the channel were connected parts of the same diary.

<Image index={2} group open />

## Releases, explanations, and compatibility

The IsLands 0.4.1 Alpha post combines release notes with a personal explanation of the update schedule and Linux compatibility work. It shows the format I wanted: useful details without hiding the unfinished parts of development.

<Image index={3} group open />

## Inviting the community to contribute music

In October 2025 I invited subscribers to submit music for consideration in the game.

<Image index={4} group open />

## A glimpse of the next update

Not every post was a long changelog. A preview of new island generation and displacement could show an experiment while it was still taking shape, keeping the development conversation alive between releases.

<Image index={5} group open />

## The Microsoft Store milestone

The January 2026 post documents bringing the current game version to Microsoft Store, including packaging difficulties and save-data adjustments. It is a practical behind-the-scenes record, not just a finished announcement.

<Image index={6} group open />

## Five hundred people and more polls

On 24 January 2026 I thanked the community for reaching 500 people, shared a music release, and described plans for more thematic updates and more frequent polls. That dated milestone sits alongside my remembered peak of around 600 and roughly 450 at closure.

<Image index={7} group open />

## Small visual improvements

A February post about inventory visuals shows the quieter side of the process: using a break from difficult technical problems to improve small details. Those in-between steps also had a place in the channel.

<Image index={8} group open />

## Playing together

The 23 February 2026 post thanks players for a shared session celebrating the opening of a Discord server. There were bugs, but there was also the experience of people playing together in the world I had been building.

<Image index={9} group open />

## Being honest about unfinished work

A March update explains why a new version was taking longer: accumulated bugs, more than a hundred fixes reported in the post, and work that ranged from small engine settings to AI-system changes. I preferred to explain the situation rather than promise an exact release date.

<Image index={10} group open />

## What feedback changed

Community feedback helped me prioritise work and encouraged me to attempt multiplayer. Discussions and playtests became part of how I developed the game.

<StoryPanel kind="feedback" group />

## Closing this chapter

I closed the channel after burning out and reassessing my relationship with publicity, media presence, and game development.
