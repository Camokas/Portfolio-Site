## Learning a second platform

I started TikTok in September 2025 by reposting my YouTube Shorts. It was convenient, but the same videos often failed to reach even half the views and engagement they had on YouTube. Around 18 January 2026, I began making separate videos specifically for TikTok, effectively working on two fronts.

<StoryPanel kind="reach" group />

## What the results meant to me

The leading TikTok reached about 82,000 views, roughly twice the 39,344 views of my leading YouTube Short in the supplied screenshots. From memory, TikTok videos generally attracted around 1.5–3 times as many views and brought more people into Telegram. That range is my recollection, not a calculated channel-wide average.

The screenshots also show that some successful TikToks predate the approximate January change of approach. The shift was gradual, not a clean boundary between two kinds of content.

<StoryPanel kind="reach" group />

## The community changed the game

Feedback pushed me to attempt multiplayer, something I might not have dared to do alone, and to add sound. Bug reports, compatibility feedback, and requests for new blocks and creatures also shaped IsLands. The community was part of the development conversation, not just an audience.

For transparency: substantial AI assistance became part of IsLands development roughly a quarter of the way into the project's timeline. This describes when that workflow began, not the fraction of the game made by AI. It is separate from video production.

## Audience snapshot

As of 31 October 2026, I reported around 1.1K TikTok followers and 41K likes. These are later, rounded snapshots, not live counters or end-of-period totals.
