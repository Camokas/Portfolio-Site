## A development diary that became a community

From June 2025 to October 2026, I turned development into an ongoing video diary. I published 107 Shorts, mainly about IsLands, alongside a smaller collection of horizontal videos: music for my projects, a New Year review of the year, and a Misform trailer for itch.io.

I tried to release a Short every day or every other day. I genuinely loved the process: finding a story in an update, learning to explain it clearly, improving the edit, and then watching views and subscribers grow. Reading comments made development feel less solitary: there were people waiting for the next step.

## From Shorts to Telegram

Shorts became an effective way to bring people into my Telegram community, especially around IsLands. The channel is now closed: it had roughly 450 subscribers when it closed and peaked at around 600. Its value was more than a number: viewers discussed updates, reported bugs, and helped shape the project.

## A record of the journey

I also made a New Year video looking back over the year. That video is not included in the supplied media set, but it belongs to the wider story of the channel: documenting progress, sharing the work behind the games, and learning to communicate with an audience.


## The community changed the game

Feedback pushed me to attempt multiplayer, something I might not have dared to do alone, and to add sound. Bug reports, compatibility feedback, and requests for new blocks and creatures also shaped IsLands. The community was part of the development conversation, not just an audience.

For transparency: substantial AI assistance became part of IsLands development roughly a quarter of the way into the project's timeline. This describes when that workflow began, not the fraction of the game made by AI. It is separate from video production.

## Audience snapshot

As of 31 October 2026, I reported 1,020 YouTube subscribers. This is a later snapshot, not the count at the end of the project period, and it does not update automatically.
